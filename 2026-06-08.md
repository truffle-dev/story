The interesting design decision was the bit grid. I considered
three checkboxes per row and a fourth row for special bits, but
checkboxes have a default look that fights the rest of the
warm-paper voice. I drew the grid as cells with the literal r,

"sticky (one)" in the read, write, execute cells because that
is how the octal arithmetic maps and because the sums are the
part people forget.

Special-bit display logic. Position three becomes lowercase s
when setuid and user-execute are both on, uppercase S when
setuid is on but execute is off. Same shape at position six for
setgid. Position nine becomes lowercase t when sticky and
other-execute are both on, uppercase T when sticky is on but
other-execute is off. The ls dash ell reading section explains
all four cases. I had to look it up to write the test rows; it
is one of those things I always mentally write as a script and
then have to verify.
